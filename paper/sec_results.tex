\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Responses to controlled degradation}\label{sec:class}

Eight hundred pairs make the answer independent of any reference model: each pair holds
the same broadcast content before and after a deterministic degradation, so the member a
quality score should rank higher is fixed by construction. Figure~S4 of the supplement gives every operation on every degradation type as a single
heatmap ordered by worst cell, and Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} in
Section~\ref{sec:synthesis} regroups the same accuracies by the work that uses each one.

\textbf{Every one of the fifteen operations with a declared direction ranks the degraded
copy above the original} on at least one degradation type, and thirteen do so under white noise, where five of them are wrong on all one hundred pairs: gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) contrast, entropy, Laplacian
variance, gradient orientation and gradient
magnitude. Two operations, hue count and saliency area, are wrong on more than half
the eight types, and a third is wrong on exactly half. The two exceptions to the noise pattern, hue count and edge
distribution, fail elsewhere instead: hue count inverts on five of the eight types and
edge distribution on H.264 and JPEG.

White noise concentrates the failures. These operations are statistics of local structure
such as gradient magnitude, edge density, entropy, contrast and descriptor counts, and
additive noise raises such statistics, so an operation reading more structure as more
quality prefers the noisier frame. Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} measures one instance of that
reasoning for the deployed BoVW score, which is not one of the fifteen: there the statistic
is a descriptor count, and the sign of the score difference follows the sign of the count
difference in 797 of the 800 pairs. \textbf{Across the fifteen the outcome recurs without that demonstration.}

On material where the better member is known, the reading tested here inverts: carrying
these operations into a selection stage and treating a higher value as better technical
quality is not supported by their response to controlled damage.

\textbf{The same 800 pairs place the deployed operation and the no-reference models}
(Table~\ref{tab:constructed}), with the reference model out of the decision: each pair
contains the same content before and after a known degradation. The
deployed operation is correct on 65.1\% of 800 pairs. LIQE reaches 99.5\%; the
paired difference is $+34.4$ percentage points, segment-block 95\% CI
$[+31.1,+37.7]$. Every evaluated NR-IQA candidate exceeds the deployed score.

No method ties on these pairs. Excluding darkening and desaturation leaves 600
comparisons and does not repair the deployed operation: its accuracy is 63.7\%,
whereas LIQE reaches 99.8\% and Q-Align 100\% (Table~S12). 

The average conceals the diagnostic result. SIFT+BoVW is at or above 88\% on
five types but selects the degraded copy in 92\% of white-noise pairs and 94\%
of JPEG pairs. Within segment, its score increases with severity for noise
($\rho=+.720$, CI $[+.455,+.925]$) and JPEG ($+.640$, CI
$[+.395,+.850]$). The mechanism is directly visible in the saved descriptor
counts. The degraded copy has more descriptors in 92\% of noise pairs (mean
change $+469$) and 95\% of JPEG pairs (mean change $+289$), with mean noise
count increasing at every severity level. These measurements are consistent
with noise and blocking creating detectable extrema that increase the count
channel in Eq.~\ref{eq:factor}; structure-removing distortions instead happen
to move count in the direction of fidelity. The score is therefore responsive,
but not consistently to quality (Table~S12).

Q-Align is correct on 98.2\% of these pairs (95\% CI $[97.2,99.1]$), which
supports its technical-degradation competence on this broadcast content. The exact downstream DOVER technical branch (DOVER-T) output
reaches 97.6\% ($[96.1,99.0]$), or 99.8\% ($[99.5,100]$) without darkening and
desaturation. The 19
extractor substitutions average 44.9\% (CI $[41.0,48.9]$) and remain dominated
by their descriptor count, corroborating the mechanism while refuting the
registered prediction that fixed feature maps would force chance performance.
The full prediction and overlay audits are in Table~S12.
\subsection{Mechanistic diagnosis}\label{sec:mechanism}

The deployed inverse document frequency uses the vocabulary size,
$\mathrm{idf}_{\mathrm{dep}}(v)=\log_{10}(K/\mathrm{df}(v))$, whereas canonical
TF-IDF uses the number of documents,
$\mathrm{idf}_{\mathrm{can}}(v)=\log_{10}(N/\mathrm{df}(v))$. Their relation is
exact:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{dep}}(s)=S_{\mathrm{can}}(s)+D_s\log_{10}(K/N),
\label{eq:idfrelation}
\end{equation}
where $D_s$ is the number of descriptors. The formulations therefore differ by
an additive count term, not by rescaling. The identity holds within
$3\times10^{-11}$ on all 93 segment slots. At cluster scale
$N\in\{2,3,4\}$, so the additional term rewards count and contributes a median
98.3\% of the deployed score. On a database of approximately $10^4$ documents,
the term is negative and reverses the count ordering.

The more general factorization separates the two available channels, and
Figure~S1 of the supplement shows the two together with the evidence that fixes their
relative weight:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}_i=\sum_v\mathrm{tf}_{iv}\mathrm{idf}_v
=D_i\underbrace{\sum_v p_i(v)\mathrm{idf}_v}_{\overline{\mathrm{idf}}_i}.
\label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
Thus a score can vary through how many descriptors the extractor finds and
through their mean IDF weight. The relative contribution of the two channels is measured empirically.

On KonIQ-10k, the coefficient of variation is .938 for $D_i$ and .098 for mean
IDF weight. The canonical score consequently correlates .998 with descriptor
count. Its association with human scores is statistically equivalent, within
the operational $\pm.02$ margin, to that of keypoint density:
$\rho^{*}=.447$ for both, with score-to-keypoint correlation .998. Steiger's
$Z=.199$ is below the Bonferroni-corrected critical value of 3.95, and both
one-sided equivalence tests reject. KADID gives the same association-level
result under reference-block resampling: .125 for canonical BoVW and .123 for
keypoint density, paired difference $+.0024$, 95\% CI
$[-.0019,+.0084]$. This is equivalence of measured MOS/DMOS association, not
identity of the scores or constructs. The deployed formulation is almost exactly the sign-reversed
count on the large databases ($-.9999$ on KonIQ, $-.9998$ on KADID and
$-.9947$ on CLIVE). In the deployment regime its own descriptor count remains
dominant, $\rho(S_{\mathrm{dep}},D)=+.994$.

Replacing SIFT with 19 convolutional extractors leaves the external ceiling where it was.
Their mean $|\rho|$ is .090 on KonIQ and .112 on CLIVE, with no value
above .238 on either database; KADID ranges from .156 to .418. Fixed spatial
feature maps remove per-image count variation on the image databases, leaving
the rarity channel measured by the ablation. On broadcast segments, however,
the upstream key-frame selector varies the number of retained frames, so it
reintroduces count variation: across the 19 variants,
$\rho(S,D)=+.981$ to $+.997$. Exchanging the extractor therefore changes individual
choices without making the ordering a better predictor of quality.

Simple normalization does not repair the mechanism. Dividing by descriptor
count exposes the weak mean-IDF-weight channel already measured by the ablation. Dividing
by frame count is also mismatched to the deployed operation because its internal
key-frame selector makes descriptor count nearly independent of duration. On the
42 clusters, frame normalization changes the uncalibrated DOVER two-branch-sum
output only from 47.4\% to 50.6\% and induces an inverse duration association
($\rho=-.52$). Full normalization and pooling results are in Tables~S6 and S9.

\subsection{Performance of literature-derived composites and source-specified
rules}\label{sec:combinations}

Nineteen attribute combinations were collected from the summarization literature, twelve
of them with more than one term. Each went through the screening rule of
Section~\ref{sec:screen} under two aggregations chosen here, a $z$-score mean and, as a
stronger variant, a ridge model fitted on the whole of KADID-10k; the rules the sources
themselves specify are reproduced separately, later in this section.

\textbf{One of the nineteen clears the sensitivity gate.} The composite of colourfulness, Laplacian variance and local binary pattern (LBP)
uniformity
reaches a within-reference mean of $.524$ on 17 of the 25 distortion types, above the $.50$
and 15-type requirement that no individual operation met. Its transfer then collapses: on
KonIQ-10k the same composite reaches $\rho^{*}=.061$, and fitting the ridge on the entirety
of KADID before applying it to KonIQ moves that to $.067$, against a requirement of $.65$.
The first figure is a within-reference severity response on KADID and the second an
association with opinion scores on KonIQ, defined in Section~\ref{sec:screen}; the drop is
what the second gate exists to detect in a combination calibrated on one severity ladder.
The remaining 18 combinations do not reach even the first gate. \textbf{No combination
evaluated here satisfies the protocol's two damage requirements at once.}

The two aggregations above are our choices. Several sources do specify how their terms
combine, and those specifications were implemented and put through the same bench; two of
them prescribe a rule over the same attribute vector as implemented here, whose adaptations
are identified in Table~S25 of the supplement, so the comparison isolates the rule. Ke~\citep{ke2006highlevel} rejects the linear option in the text, writing
that ``a naive method might
use a weighted linear combination of the quality metrics'' but that ``the quality metrics we
defined are not linear'', and uses a naive Bayes classifier instead. What is reproduced
here is that rule and not Ke's fitted model: the classifier is Gaussian naive Bayes over that
same vector, but its labels are this bench's original and degraded members rather than
the professional and snapshot photographs Ke trained on, and its probabilities are estimated
here. Validated leaving one segment out it reaches \regraKe{} per cent against the
\regraMedia{} per cent of the unweighted mean, a fall of \regraKeQueda{} points, so the loss
is attributable to the combination rule and not to a change of terms.

Srinivas~\citep{srinivas2016rank} takes edge distribution, hue count and contrast from Ke by
citation and colourfulness from a different source, and specifies the other of the two
alternatives: his
quality score is ``the average of all these individual scores'', so \textbf{the mean the
later literature executes is not a simplification of that source but the rule it gives}.
His weights ``directly proportional to the standard deviation'' belong to another level of
the same paper, combining four score types of which this bench carries one. Applying that
weighting to the five attributes is an adaptation of ours, not a reproduction; as a
sensitivity control it reaches \regraSrinivasAdapt{} per cent and gives edge distribution a
weight of \regraPesoZero{} against the mean's \regraPesoMedia{}.

One source specifies a complete module, reproduced here as published. The Qaroush~\citep{qaroush2025diversity} visual-attention objective is the unweighted sum of five named terms
(Eq.~20); three were already in the inventory, and the other two, the contrast term of
Eq.~8 and mean motion intensity, were implemented and measured on the same bench. Over the
seven degradation types that are not aligned with any member by construction, desaturation
being excluded because it acts directly on the colourfulness term, the module
scores \vaModulo{} per cent while its best single term alone scores \vaMelhorMembro{}. It keeps the failure signature of the isolated operations, answering \vaRuido{} per cent of
white-noise and \vaJpeg{} per cent of JPEG pairs, and its contrast term contributes
\vaCT{} per cent alone, which the next paragraph explains.

A second complete module behaves the same way. Korolkov~\citep{korolkov2025keyframe}
specifies
$0.7\,z(\text{sharpness})+0.3\,z(\text{brightness})$ with both weights and the $z$-score
normalization given in the text. The normalization here is computed over all candidates of
the bench, that is the original and every degraded member of each segment, which is the
population a selector would rank; reproduced on the \koN{} of \koDe{} segments for which both terms
were measured and over the seven types that exclude darkening, which acts directly on the
brightness term, it scores \koModulo{} per cent where its own sharpness term alone scores
\koMelhorMembro{}, and the paired contrast over the same decisions is
\koContraste{} points with a segment-block interval of \koIC{} that touches zero, so at this
sample size the shortfall is not established. \textbf{For the Qaroush module the same
contrast is \vaContraste{} points with an interval of \vaIC{} that excludes zero: there the
published module is measurably worse than its own best component.} The subset departs from the 20-segment result by \koPortao{} points on one
degradation type, so it replicates the direction rather than estimating the size again.

In two sources the published expression carries the opposite sign to the property the
surrounding text names for it. The contrast term of the
Qaroush visual-attention objective is anti-correlated by construction with contrast, at
$-1.0000$ over a sweep in which contrast rises by construction; the brightness term of the
Korolkov module is stated to penalize over-exposed frames, but the mean luminance it
specifies rises monotonically with exposure and enters the module with a positive weight,
so brighter always scores higher. \textbf{In both cases the expression contradicts the
property the same paragraph claims for it}, and in both the contradiction is visible in the
equation before any measurement (Table~S19 of the supplement).

\subsection{External validation and screening}\label{sec:screen_results}

The constructed pairs fix the better member by construction, but they use eight
degradations we chose. A public database with human opinion scores asks the same question
independently, and applies to the individual operations, their composites and the
no-reference models alike. All sixteen operations go through a rule with two damage
requirements, responding to distortion severity within a reference image and retaining that
response on databases the method was never fitted on, plus one in-domain requirement.
Table~S24 of the supplement reports each family's best value on each axis separately, so a
family fails an axis only when its best member fails it.

\textbf{No operation clears either damage gate, and the margin is not narrow.} The best
within-reference mean among the fifteen is $.298$ against a requirement of $.50$, and the
best lowest-unseen correlation is $.139$ against $.65$; the higher $.218$ in that column
belongs to spatial information, which carries no verdict. Four operations are
negative on the first requirement, which means their response runs against the direction
their own use assumes. \pisoTabPassam{} of the \pisoTabPop{} operations in Table~S17
that declare a direction and have a downstream choice defined do clear the in-domain
requirement, and the bracketed lower bounds show how much of each pass survives its
interval: the weakest is \pisoMenorLo{}. Several operations therefore agree with the reference model on unmodified material while
failing both external damage requirements.

Within-reference KADID sensitivity separates the deployed operation from
specialized quality methods. Its mean direction-aligned correlation is $-.077$
(95\% CI $[-.124,-.030]$), with only 5 of 25 types above .50. LIQE,
QualiCLIP and CLIP-IQA$+$ reach .807, .849 and .808, respectively, each with 22
types above .50. Most remaining NR-IQA candidates also respond broadly, with
means from .607 to .733.

Table~S21 applies the same complete-case rule to every method.
Released-weight exposure is visible because an in-database correlation is not a
generalization result. The generalization column reports the lowest signed
$\rho^{*}$ over every unseen database, rather than using an absolute value that
could hide inversion.

Three methods are eligible: LIQE, QualiCLIP and CLIP-IQA$+$. QualiCLIP has the largest
sensitivity mean and LIQE the largest generalization margin, so the three form an
eligibility set rather than a ranking. The set is unchanged when the generalization
threshold varies from .60 to .65 or the sensitivity coverage cut varies from 12 to 22
types (Table~S10); at .55 MUSIQ is added, and at .70 only LIQE remains.

The database-level values show why the universal unseen-database clause matters.
LIQE obtains $\rho^{*}=.723$ on KADID and .865 on CLIVE; QualiCLIP obtains .665
and .757; CLIP-IQA$+$ obtains .654 and .805. TOPIQ and MUSIQ are strong on the
unseen authentic database (.811 and .789) but fall to .511 and .556 on KADID.
DBCNN and HyperIQA similarly fall from .756 and .750 on CLIVE to .484 and .462
on KADID. An existential rule would admit methods whose favorable authentic
result masks failure under controlled synthetic distortion. Conversely, the
three eligible methods pass the floor with overlapping topic-block intervals.

The floor axis shows the same overlap. LIQE agrees with Q-Align
on 32 of 42 clusters ($\kappa_{\mathrm{uniform}}=.551$, 95\% CI
$[.284,.786]$); QualiCLIP agrees on 28 ($.371$, $[.143,.597]$); and
CLIP-IQA$+$ on 25 ($.236$, $[.034,.435]$). The deployed operation agrees on 21,
only 1.3 decisions above the uniform expectation, and its normalized interval
crosses chance ($.056$, $[-.308,.412]$). Results on the 15 human-annotated redundancy clusters
are in Tables~S7--S8, kept apart from the operational 42-cluster set.

\textbf{The verdict travels with the benchmark that calibrates it.} The same
sensitivity requirement, computed on the constructed broadcast pairs instead of KADID-10k,
admits six of the fifteen directionally evaluated operations, and none meets the
requirement on both benchmarks. Spatial information clears it there too, and is reported
separately as a diagnostic control that receives no screening verdict. The two measure damage response by different routes, 25
synthetic types on still photographs against eight types in the deployment domain, and the
rule was applied at the same fraction of types on each.

The full P.1401 triad and database-level correlations are in Tables~S4 and S15.
The latter reports training-database contrasts, and conditioning on detectable keypoint density strongly attenuates the BoVW score (Table~S16). Cost is also secondary to
eligibility: in the common environment the deployed operation takes 182~s per
cluster and LIQE 1.9~s (Table~S14).

\subsection{What the choice of operation does to the pipeline}\label{sec:menu}

The preceding sections measure each operation against material with a known answer or
against human opinion. This one asks a different question, and it needs no reference at
all: if the pipeline keeps every stage fixed and only the visual-selection module is exchanged,
\textbf{do the operations pick the same segments?}

They do not. Fourteen operations for which a downstream choice is defined produce eleven
distinct summaries over the same 42 redundancy clusters. Only two groups coincide, and
both coincide for a reason that has nothing to do with agreement: the three brightness
variants compute the same quantity under different names, and the two colourfulness
implementations are the same Hasler expression reached through different source works.
Every one of the 42 clusters contains at least two operations that disagree, and the
average cluster draws $2.21$ distinct choices from the fourteen.

Pairwise, the median agreement between two operations is \dupMediana{} per cent against a chance
floor of $47.0$, and \textbf{37 of the 91 pairs agree below chance}; Figure~S3 of the
supplement shows both distributions. Collapsing those duplicate computations leaves \dupDistintas{} distinct ones and
\dupPares{} pairs, the same median of \dupMediana{} and \dupAbaixo{} below chance.
\textbf{What this establishes is that the choice of operation changes which representatives
the pipeline selects, with every other stage fixed.} No source work evaluates its operation
against the others on common material.

How far the decisions move is the next question, and it applies first to the alternatives
\emph{the literature itself already offers}: a practitioner reading this literature is
choosing among them, not among no-reference models. Figure~\ref{fig:jusante} answers it by
replacing the deployed operation with each of the fourteen in turn, holding every other
stage and the decoded frames fixed.

\input{fig_jusante}

The fourteen span from \jusMax{} to \jusMin{} per cent agreement with the
deployed operation, so the choice among published operations rewrites between
\jusReescreveMin{} and \jusReescreveMax{} per cent of what the stage outputs.
The median operation retains \jusMediana{} per cent against a uniform-chance
floor of \jusPiso{}, and \jusAbaixo{} operations (\jusAbaixoNomes{}) retain
less than chance. Of the fourteen, \jusAuditadas{} belong to the main inventory and all \jusAuditadas{} fail
the screening of Section~\ref{sec:screen_results}; the remaining \jusSoJusante{} are
evaluated only in this substitution and carry no screening verdict. The last two columns of
Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} link those \jusAuditadas{} to their other measurements, and
Table~S19 records the complete coverage. Whichever of these operations a system adopts, it
inherits a different summary, and the published record offers no measurement that would let
an implementer prefer one. Because these percentages use one frame per second while
Table~S20 scores each complete operation under its own sampling, the two must not be read
across.

A validated no-reference model in the same slot is the complementary case. LIQE is used for
the direct operation substitution because it has the largest
generalization margin among the three eligible methods and near-ceiling accuracy
on the constructed pairs. With the same ten uniformly spaced frames and mean
pooling, SIFT+BoVW and LIQE still differ in 16 of 42 clusters. The LIQE--SIFT
DOVER-T contrast is $+27.1$ range points (topic CI $[5.6,47.8]$; hierarchical
$[2.4,52.5]$) and $+.0074$ raw, with both intervals positive. Table~S20 reports the complete-operation comparison and all eligible methods.

Replacing the deployed visual-quality operation with the LIQE-based operation
changes 22 of 42 representatives. On the primary DOVER technical branch, the
global-achievable-range point estimate rises from 50.5\% to 76.0\%, a paired
increase of $+25.5$ points. Its topic-block interval is $[-12.1,+54.9]$ and its
hierarchical interval is $[-17.2,+59.6]$; neither confirms a population-level
effect. The raw branch-score difference is $+.0069$ (topic-block
$[-.0028,+.0178]$; hierarchical $[-.0041,+.0197]$), while mean per-cluster
position and selected-maximum rate increase by 7.2 and 4.8 points, respectively.

No cluster has zero DOVER-T range. Excluding the smallest 10\% or 25\% of ranges
gives global-range differences of $+25.9$ and $+28.1$ points, so the positive
point estimate is not generated solely by near-zero ranges. As a construct
sensitivity analysis, the uncalibrated sum of DOVER's technical and aesthetic
branches reproduces the earlier $+30.3$-point contrast, but that sum is neither
the official standardized fused score nor a purely technical measure
(Table~S11).

The reference-model columns give positive point estimates for the substitution, with
overlapping intervals across the eligible set. \textbf{The supported end-to-end conclusion
is that the operation substitution materially changes the selected evidence.}

\subsection{The audit read by source work}\label{sec:synthesis}

Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} brings together the roles the sources document and the results of
the implementations evaluated here, organized by work and component.

\input{tab_sintese}


Three things are legible there. The first is that \emph{the same computation carries
different claims}. Laplacian variance appears twice because two works use it, and the roles
are not interchangeable: one applies it as a threshold that discards blurred frames, the
other as the dominant weighted term of a keyframe score. The same statistic appears in both bands, each row on the
sample its band names, and what was measured is the statistic as a scorer. It answers blur on every pair and
inverts the ordering under noise. \textbf{That characterizes the statistic, not the published
filter}: a scorer can order a pair correctly while a threshold keeps or drops both members,
and this bench did not run the threshold.

The second is visible in the two module rows, whose means sit inside a band of red cells:
an aggregate can pass while the types on which a quality score would reject material fail.
Section~\ref{sec:combinations} gives those figures with their intervals, computed on the
types not aligned with each module's own construction and therefore lower than the
all-eight means in the table.

The third is the clause that closes each band, read from the source itself. Of the
\valComPdf{} works whose full text is in our archive, \valPropriedade{} test a measure against human
judgment of the property it names, and in both the object tested is a whole:
\citet{ke2006highlevel} evaluates the \emph{photo-quality classifier} against community
ratings, not each of its four features alone, and \citet{hasler2003colorfulness} fits its
\emph{colourfulness index} to a psychophysical scaling experiment. The remaining sources report summary-level evaluation rather than isolated validation of
the visual component, and \citet{korolkov2025keyframe} fixes its weights without testing
them; Table~S26 of the supplement gives, source by source, the excerpt that fixes each
reading. \textbf{Summary-level evaluation does not isolate the visual component's validity as a
technical-quality measure.}

Two of the four operations taken from the diversity-and-coverage work serve coverage and
diversity there, not quality: the \emph{here:} clause on each band is what separates the
reading tested from the job the source gives the operation.
