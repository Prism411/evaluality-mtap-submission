\section{Audit design}\label{sec:method}

The object of the audit is a class of operations, not a system. Sixteen visual operations
that the summarization literature applies at the selection stage are reimplemented from
the equations their source works publish, fifteen of them closed-form and one requiring
a learned saliency map, and every one of them is put through a common protocol
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Ten no-reference quality models and one deployed pipeline
component are carried through that protocol, with the tests available for each
implementation recorded in the coverage matrix, so that the class can be read against
something.

Four forms of evidence enter. Construction-defined degradation pairs in the broadcast
domain fix the better member without a reference model and give the primary test.
External validation against human opinion scores on three public databases gives the
severity response and its transfer. A mechanistic analysis, available in closed form for
the one operation that is a published pipeline component, supplies the explanation. An
operation-level substitution in a working pipeline measures how far the choice moves real
decisions. A method is eligible only if it responds to controlled degradation, generalizes
to every unseen opinion database and exceeds a reference-model agreement floor in the
deployment domain.

\textbf{Three categories of implementation are kept apart throughout.} An operation is
\emph{reproduced} when the source publishes an equation this article evaluates unchanged;
\emph{adapted} when the source defines the term over a region, a sequence or a color space
that the single-frame protocol requires reducing, in which case the reduction is stated;
and \emph{exploratory} when no source declares a direction, in which case the operation is
reported but excluded from any pass-or-fail count. Two of the sixteen are singled out on
this basis and both are stated wherever they appear: the International Telecommunication Union Telecommunication Standardization Sector
(ITU-T) spatial information index
declares no quality direction and is therefore excluded from every accuracy count, leaving
fifteen; and saliency area is the one operation whose source requires a learned map rather
than a closed-form expression, the other fifteen being closed-form. Table~S17
carries the per-operation detail. This separation matters because a negative result about a
form the protocol had to reduce is a weaker claim than one about a form the source
publishes outright, and the two should not be summed.

Table~S23 of the supplement carries the correspondence: for each operation, the source
work, the formula as that source states it, the function that computes it, the direction
it declares and the reference model used on the in-domain column, with the results file
named in its footnote. \textbf{Every operation name comes from that mapping}, and every value in the tables and
figures of this article is generated from the measurement files it points to, with the
supplementary tables transcribed from the same files.
Sixteen operations are computed. Fifteen carry a direction their source declares and are the
ones any pass-or-fail count refers to; spatial information is the sixteenth and carries
none. The deployed BoVW score is \emph{not} one of
the sixteen: it is the pipeline component under audit, screened by the same protocol and
reported alongside them, which is why the tables show seventeen rows where both appear.

\subsection{Provenance audit and reproduction of the published rules}\label{sec:prov}

Each operation was traced to the work the literature cites for it, and three things were
recorded from that work rather than inferred: the objective it states, the role the
operation has inside that objective, and a verbatim excerpt fixing both. One criterion was
applied to all sixteen. \textbf{An operation is a stand-alone index in its source when it
alone produces the number that orders the candidates there}; it is not when it enters as a
term of a sum, a feature of a classifier, one block of a spliced representation vector, or
one signal in a filter that discards frames. Where a source
names a numbered objective, the equation is cited so that each term's membership is
checkable. \textbf{By that criterion \autAutonomas{} of the \autVerificadas{} is a
stand-alone index in its source}, the Hasler colourfulness expression, and it indexes
colourfulness rather than quality; the rest enter as classifier features, terms of a sum,
blocks of a spliced vector, or signals in a discard filter.

A fourth thing was recorded from every source whose full text is in our archive: the
evidence the work itself reports for its visual criterion. For each we noted \textbf{the
object the source evaluates}, whether an individual attribute, a combined module or the
final summary; \textbf{the reference it evaluates against}, whether human ratings of the
named property, summary-level evaluation, or an internal ablation; and a verbatim
excerpt fixing both. \textbf{Ten of the eleven source works were read in full for this dimension}; the
exception is \citet{xu2024property}, one of three sources for contrast variance, whose full
text is not in our archive and which is therefore \textbf{marked as not assessed rather than
as reporting no validation}, since the two are different claims. Table~S26 of the supplement
carries the classification with its excerpts.

Where a source also states \emph{how} its terms combine, that specification was implemented
as written and put through the paired test of Section~\ref{sec:controlled} with the terms,
frames and pooling held fixed, so the rule is the only thing that differs. Two limits are
declared rather than worked around: a source stating no rule cannot be reproduced this way,
and a module term needing a quantity the paired bench does not carry, such as a frame's
distance from a cluster center, is reported as a structural observation, not a measurement.

Table~S17 states, for each of the sixteen operations, the objective its
source declares and the role it has there. The reading tested here is the one the summarization literature applies when it carries
these operations into a selection stage: one frame, no model, higher is better technical
quality.

Table~S25 of the supplement links each source-defined role to the implementation
evaluated here, including changes of input, aggregation and score direction, with the
verbatim excerpt that fixes each role. Every one of the \autVerificadas{} operations carries such an excerpt, spatial
information included: ITU-T~P.910 defines it as \emph{``a measure that generally indicates
the amount of spatial detail of a picture''}, higher for more spatially complex scenes, and
uses it to select test sequences~\citep{itu_p910}.
Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} reports the corresponding results by source work and component.

The four operations taken from Qaroush et al.~\citep{qaroush2025diversity} show the distinction at its sharpest: that
source states four numbered objectives and its four terms do not share one. Gradient
magnitude and saliency enter visual attention (Eq.~20), but entropy enters segment coverage
(Eq.~21) and gradient orientation segment diversity (Eq.~22), so \qaroushFora{} of the
\qaroushDe{} are not quality terms in their own source at all. Two works that adopt the same operation can also carry it
differently: Laplacian variance is a discard threshold in Cakmak et
al.~\citep{cakmak2025tripss} and a weighted term in Korolkov~\citep{korolkov2025keyframe},
and in neither is it the score that orders candidates.

Jeong et al.~\citep{jeong2016static} show the same distance in another form: contrast there
is pixel variance, but it enters a \emph{badness} beside blur and skew inside
$a_i=d_i\sqrt{c_i+b_i+g(c_i+b_i+s_i)}$, whose leading factor is the Mahalanobis distance of
the frame from its cluster center. \textbf{The component audited here is pixel variance on
its own}, read in the increasing direction.


\subsection{Data and units of analysis}\label{sec:data}

The external benchmark uses KADID-10k, KonIQ-10k and LIVE In the Wild (CLIVE).
KADID-10k contains 10,125 distorted images derived from 81 references, with 25
distortion types at five levels and Differential Mean Opinion Scores
(DMOS)~\citep{lin2019kadid}. KonIQ-10k contains 10,073 authentically distorted
images, approximately 1.2 million ratings and Mean Opinion Scores
(MOS)~\citep{hosu2020koniq}; CLIVE
contains 1,162 mobile photographs with authentic distortions
~\citep{ghadiyaram2016clive}. No no-reference model is retrained or fine-tuned. Two \emph{combination} rules are fitted over the operations' outputs alone: the ridge variant of Section~\ref{sec:combinations} and the classifier reproducing Ke's rule, validated leaving one segment out.

The deployment analysis uses the redundancy stage produced by HSMVideoSumm over
19 of its 21 news topics: 42 multi-member clusters, 93 segment slots and 83
distinct segments. The other two topics contain no multi-member cluster. These
clusters are operational inputs, not proof of equal semantic utility. The
original human redundancy annotations cover only 15 clusters and are retained
as a supplementary sensitivity analysis (Tables~S1, S7, S8 and S11); the
42-cluster set is primary because it is the set encountered by the deployed
pipeline.

Still-image scorers receive frames sampled uniformly at 1 frame per second and
are pooled by the arithmetic mean,
\begin{equation}
S(s)=\frac{1}{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\phi(f_i),
\label{eq:meanpool}
\end{equation}
where $\phi$ is the per-frame score. The deployed operation instead applies its
own key-frame selector, quantizes retained descriptors into a vocabulary of
$K=300$ visual words and sums
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{BoVW}}(s)=\sum_i\sum_{v=1}^{K}
\mathrm{tf}(v,f_i)\,\mathrm{idf}(v).
\label{eq:bovw}
\end{equation}
Sampling, scoring and pooling jointly define the operation occupying the
visual-selection stage, so the primary comparison evaluates complete operations
while holding every other stage fixed. A matched-input control additionally gives
SIFT+BoVW and LIQE the same fixed set of at most ten uniformly spaced 1-fps
frames and uses arithmetic-mean pooling for both. It thus isolates scorer
identity within a matched-input regime; full scores and alternative
normalizations are in Tables~S6, S9 and S11.

Q-Align is an in-domain reference model rather than a candidate
~\citep{wu2024qalign}. \textbf{The two families are scored against different references on
the in-domain axis}: the ten no-reference models are compared with Q-Align, and the
summarization operations with LIQE, so that column compares each family with a reference
outside its own kind and never across the two. The
\texttt{q-future/one-align} checkpoint is run with the \texttt{quality} task,
\texttt{image} input, 4-bit NormalFloat4 (NF4) double quantization and 16-bit floating-point (FP16)
compute, then pooled by Eq.~\ref{eq:meanpool}. It was among the strongest public NR-IQA models when the
inventory was frozen, is architecturally distinct from the candidates, and is
13.7 times slower than LIQE under the measured setup; it is therefore reserved
as a reference model rather than screened as an on-line candidate.

The substitution study measures changes of selection and model-reference scores of the
output; \textbf{it does not measure human preference} for the resulting summaries. DOVER is used only as an output-sensitivity reference and does not participate
in screening~\citep{wu2023dover}. The official \texttt{DOVER.pth} checkpoint and
\texttt{dover.yml} \texttt{val-l1080p} views are used (Table~S13). Its raw
technical branch is primary; the uncalibrated technical--aesthetic sum is only a
sensitivity analysis, and neither the official standardized fusion nor DOVER++
is computed. The same technical output is tested on the 800 constructed pairs,
using a common 5-fps MP4 wrapper and paired fragment seed 42. Because DOVER was
developed for user-generated video, its output is not treated as broadcast-domain
human ground truth.

Score and target directions are declared before analysis. Direction-aligned
correlation is $\rho^{*}=s_m s_y\rho$, where $s_m=+1$ for a higher-is-better
method and $-1$ for a lower-is-better method, while $s_y=+1$ when a higher
target means better quality and $-1$ when it means worse quality. All opinion
targets used here, including KADID's released \texttt{dmos} field, are
higher-is-better; degradation level is higher-is-worse. A negative $\rho^{*}$
therefore denotes a genuine directional inversion rather than a scale
convention.

\subsection{Controlled degradation and constructed truth}\label{sec:controlled}

KADID sensitivity is computed without treating derivatives of the same source
as independent. For each distortion type, Spearman correlation between score
and level is computed within each of the 81 references and averaged across
references. Confidence intervals use $10^4$ block-bootstrap resamples of the
reference images with seed 42.

The broadcast experiment supplies a second controlled test. Eight degradations
are applied at five levels: Gaussian blur, white noise, darkening, JPEG
compression, low-bitrate H.264 transcoding, a
$720\mathrm{p}\rightarrow480\mathrm{p}\rightarrow720\mathrm{p}$ round trip,
horizontal motion blur and progressive desaturation. The last uses
$S'_{\ell}=\operatorname{clip}(\alpha_{\ell}S,0,255)$ with
$\alpha_{\ell}\in\{0.75,0.55,0.35,0.18,0.05\}$. H.264 is applied to the frame
sequence; other transformations are frame-based.

Twenty of the 83 broadcast segments are sampled without replacement by a seeded
simple random draw (seed 42); they represent 13 of the 19 operational topics
(Table~S2). Pairing each unmodified segment with every degradation and level gives
$20\times8\times5=800$ binary decisions. The unmodified member is the
construction-defined higher-fidelity signal, not a human perceptual judgment.
Accuracy and paired differences are block-bootstrapped by segment
($10^4$ resamples, seed 42). A second design correlates score with degradation
level within each segment--type pair. Overlay strata, the complete registered
prediction audit and parameters are reported in Tables~S2 and S12 and the
reproducibility archive. Ties count as neither correct nor incorrect and remain
in the denominator. A sensitivity analysis excludes darkening and desaturation,
whose transformations define signal change more directly than universal
perceptual worsening. These pairs test fidelity response, not preference between
distinct broadcasts.

\subsection{Screening and application inference}\label{sec:screen}

The screening rule has three conditions. Sensitivity
passes when the within-reference mean is at least .50 and at least 15 of 25
distortion types exceed .50. Generalization passes when the direction-aligned
Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) is at least .65 on \emph{every}
database absent from the released checkpoint's
opinion training. The in-domain floor is confirmed when normalized agreement
with the reference designated for that family above, Q-Align for the no-reference models
and LIQE for the operations, is above chance and the lower bound of its 95\% topic-block
bootstrap interval exceeds zero on the common 42 clusters. Cost is reported but
not thresholded. Table~S10 varies the numerical thresholds and the quantifier
over unseen databases.

For cluster $c$ with $k_c$ candidates, independent uniform choices agree with
probability $1/k_c$. The common-set floor is therefore
$\bar p_e=|C|^{-1}\sum_{c\in C}1/k_c=.470$. Normalized agreement is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{uniform}}=(p_o-\bar p_e)/(1-\bar p_e)$. This is a custom
uniform-choice correction, not Cohen's kappa. A positive point estimate is
reported separately from a confirmed pass so that a wide interval crossing
chance cannot be read as affirmative evidence.

External association uses Spearman correlation against MOS or DMOS. Confidence
intervals (CIs) for KonIQ and CLIVE use the Fisher transform; KADID intervals and paired differences
use reference-block bootstrap. Dependent correlations on the same images use
Steiger's statistic~\citep{steiger1980comparing}. The operational equivalence
margin is $|\Delta\rho^{*}|<.02$, chosen as the largest association difference
treated as practically negligible for this diagnostic rather than as a
literature-wide standard; the paired difference and interval are also reported
so stricter margins can be applied. The multiplicity family contains all 630
pairwise contrasts among 36 evaluated scores (17 methods and 19 ablations);
Bonferroni correction gives $\alpha=0.05/630$ and critical $|z|=3.95$. The full accuracy, consistency and linearity
triad after the monotonic mapping required by ITU-T P.1401 is reported in
Table~S4~\citep{itu_p1401}.

For each operation, the application selects one representative per cluster. If
$y_c$, $l_c$ and $h_c$ are the selected, lowest and highest reference scores in
cluster $c$, define $\bar y=|C|^{-1}\sum_{c\in C}y_c$ and analogously
$\bar l$ and $\bar h$. The reported global-achievable-range estimand is
\begin{equation}
100\,\frac{\bar y-\bar l}{\bar h-\bar l}.
\label{eq:globalrange}
\end{equation}
It is a ratio of means, not an average of independently rescaled clusters.
Raw branch-score differences, mean per-cluster position, selected-maximum rate,
range distributions and exclusions of the smallest 10\% and 25\% of ranges are
reported in Table~S11. Zero-range clusters are undefined for per-cluster
position and are counted explicitly. The primary interval resamples the 19
topics and retains their clusters. A hierarchical sensitivity analysis
additionally resamples clusters within each sampled topic. Both use $10^4$
paired resamples and seed 42. Human compositional preference was not collected;
the estimand is explicitly a model-reference output.

Experiments ran on one NVIDIA RTX~4060 GPU (8~GB). NR-IQA implementations use
the PyIQA toolbox~\citep{chen2022pyiqa}; SIFT uses OpenCV and the vocabulary uses
mini-batch $K$-means with $K=300$ and \texttt{random\_state=42}. Q-Align is
quantized to 4-bit NormalFloat4 with double quantization and FP16 compute to fit
the GPU~\citep{dettmers2023qlora}; the official PyIQA architecture supports
fp16, 4-bit and 8-bit inference, with NF4 used in 4-bit mode. No numerical
equivalence to fp16 is assumed. Frames
are decoded once and cached, and latency is re-measured in one software
environment after ten warm-up and sixty timed iterations. The archive includes
the scoring scripts, derived results, cluster definitions, annotations, seeds
and environment information; redistribution of source news video remains
subject to the broadcasters' terms. The final method inventory and analysis
archive were frozen on 23 August 2026. The archive reconstructs the reported statistics from
hashed score caches and exposes the inference settings.
