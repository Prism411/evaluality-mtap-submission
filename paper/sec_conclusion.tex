\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This study audited the technical-quality behavior of visual-selection operations drawn
from the summarization literature, separating the role each source defines for an operation
from the component, module or adaptation evaluated here. Across 800 controlled pairs, \textbf{every one of the fifteen operations with a declared direction ranks the degraded
copy above the original}, on at least one of eight degradation types, and thirteen do so
under white noise. None meets both external requirements, reaching \pisoMelhorSens{} in
sensitivity against \pisoLimSens{} and \pisoMelhorGen{} in generalization against
\pisoLimGen{}; neither do the nineteen evaluated combinations, of which one clears
sensitivity and then fails transfer. Three of ten no-reference models meet both. Where a source specifies how its own terms
combine, that rule was implemented as written: of the two complete modules, one scores
measurably below its own best component and the other by a margin this sample does not
resolve.

For the deployed BoVW+TF-IDF operation an exact factorization separates a descriptor count
from a mean inverse-document-frequency weight. \textbf{The count dominates in every regime measured}, the relation survives replacing
SIFT with 19 convolutional extractors, and over the 800 pairs the sign of the score
difference follows the sign of the count difference in 797. That explains the inversions under noise and JPEG for one operation; across the other
fifteen the outcome recurs as a pattern rather than a demonstrated common cause.

Within the fixed multi-video pipeline, fourteen operations produce eleven distinct
representative selections over the same 42 clusters, and replacing the deployed
operation with the LIQE-based one \textbf{changes 22 of 42 representatives}. These results
make \textbf{visual-quality scoring an explicit design decision}, to be documented and
evaluated against the requirement it is meant to serve. No human judged these segments, and a blind pairwise
judgement of real candidates, asking separately about technical quality and about the
information each preserves, remains necessary to decide whether the changed selections
yield preferred summaries.
